THE GOLDEN RULE OF BREATH:
  THE LEFT HAND NEVER KNOWS ABOUT BREATH.
  THE LEFT HAND NEVER STOPS.
  THE LEFT HAND NEVER PAUSES.
  THE LEFT HAND KEEPS TICKING THROUGH EVERY SINGLE BAR OF THE PIECE.

Breath is a RIGHT HAND phenomenon only.

When the right hand breathes:
  - It holds one long note (d=8, d=12, d=15) → the melody rests on a note
  - OR it plays a simplified sigh gesture (2-4 slow notes)
  - OR it goes completely silent for 1-2 bars
  In ALL three cases — the left hand continues its full pattern underneath.

That is a dead stop. It feels like the music crashed.

BREATH TYPES (right hand only — left hand always full pattern):

  TYPE 1 — LONG HOLD (most common):

SECTION 4 — THE 12 LAWS OF GOOD MUSIC
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

LAW 1 — IDENTITY: Every piece needs a recognisable motif — a specific rhythm + contour. Establish it in bar 1.

LAW 2 — REPETITION: Repeat the motif every 2-4 bars. The brain needs prediction to form emotional attachment. Minimum 2 exact repetitions before first variation.

LAW 3 — VARIATION: Vary ONE parameter at a time: pitch (transpose), rhythm (lengthen), register (octave), or direction (invert). Never vary everything at once.

LAW 4 — ONE VARIABLE RULE: If the left hand changes texture → right hand stays simple. If right hand is active → left hand keeps its exact pattern. Never change both hands simultaneously.

LAW 5 — TENSION/RELEASE: One chromatic note at the cadence point per 8-bar section creates more emotion than many chromatic passing tones. Use it as a leading tone pulling to the tonic.

LAW 6 — ENERGY FLOW: Plan the macro arc BEFORE writing:
  Bars 1-2:    sparse intro — left hand alone or melody enters high and sparse
  Bars 3-6:    build — develop motif, more active
  Bars 7-9:    climax — densest/highest (~70% through the piece)
  Bars 10-end: resolve — return to sparse, come home, dissolve

LAW 7 — MEMORY RECALL: Return to the exact opening motif near the end. Recognition creates emotional closure.

LAW 8 — HIERARCHY: Music works at 3 levels simultaneously:
  MICRO:  individual note choices — every note has a reason
  MESO:   2-4 bar phrases with a goal note they move toward
  MACRO:  full piece arc — one emotional story

LAW 9 — CONTRAST: At least ONE strong texture change. Dense→suddenly sparse. High register→deep bass. This moment resets listener attention. But through the contrast, the left hand still flows.

LAW 10 — EMOTIONAL TIMING: Silence in the RIGHT HAND is music. A note after breath hits harder. But the left hand keeps the time through every silence.

LAW 11 — PHRASE VARIETY: Each phrase cycle needs its own character:
  CALL-RESPONSE:    bars 1-2 state motif, bars 3-4 answer with variation
  QUESTION-ANSWER:  phrase ends unstable, next phrase resolves
  SEQUENCE:         motif moves to new pitch level — same rhythm, new root
  ECHO:             motif in higher register, sparser
  STATEMENT-REST:   motif stated, breath bar, then variation re-enters
  No two consecutive phrase cycles should use the same pattern.

LAW 12 — CODA DISSOLUTION: The ending must be physically lighter than any section before. Right hand plays sparser, slower, longer durations. Left hand can finally shift to long sustained notes (coda only — the first time it changes character). The music becomes still before it ends.
