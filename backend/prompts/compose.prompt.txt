SECTION 4 — THE 12 LAWS OF GOOD MUSIC
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

LAW 1 — IDENTITY: Every piece needs a recognisable motif — a specific rhythm + contour. Establish it in bar 1.

LAW 2 — REPETITION: Repeat the motif every 2-4 bars. The brain needs prediction to form emotional attachment. Minimum 2 exact repetitions before first variation.

LAW 3 — VARIATION: Vary ONE parameter at a time: pitch (transpose), rhythm (lengthen), register (octave), or direction (invert). Never vary everything at once.

LAW 4 — ONE VARIABLE RULE: If the left hand changes texture → right hand stays simple. If right hand does something complex → left hand stays absolutely steady. Never change both hands simultaneously.

LAW 5 — TENSION/RELEASE: One chromatic note, placed ONCE at the cadence point per 8-bar section, does more emotional work than a bar full of chromatic passing tones. Use it as a leading tone pulling to the tonic.

LAW 6 — ENERGY FLOW: Plan the macro arc BEFORE writing:
  Bars 1-2:    sparse intro — establish identity
  Bars 3-6:    build — develop motif
  Bars 7-9:    climax — densest/highest (~70% through)
  Bars 10-end: resolve — return to sparse, come home

LAW 7 — MEMORY RECALL: Return to the exact opening motif near the end. Recognition creates emotional closure.

LAW 8 — HIERARCHY: Music works at 3 levels simultaneously:
  MICRO:  individual note choices
  MESO:   2-4 bar phrases with a goal note
  MACRO:  full piece arc — one emotional story

LAW 9 — CONTRAST: At least ONE strong texture change. Dense→suddenly sparse. High register→bass. Silence after movement. This moment resets listener attention.

LAW 10 — EMOTIONAL TIMING: Silence and rests are music. A note after a rest hits harder. Delay resolution to create longing.

LAW 11 — PHRASE VARIETY: Each 2-4 bar phrase needs its own character. Choose from:
  CALL-RESPONSE:    bars 1-2 state motif, bars 3-4 answer with variation
  QUESTION-ANSWER:  phrase ends unstable, next phrase resolves
  SEQUENCE:         motif moves to new pitch level — same rhythm, new root
  ECHO:             motif in higher register, sparser
  STATEMENT-REST:   motif stated, silence, then repeated with variation
  No two consecutive phrases should use the same pattern.

LAW 12 — CODA TEXTURE CHANGE: The ending must be physically LIGHTER than any section before it. Fewer notes per bar. Longer durations. Both hands slow their density. Music becomes still before it ends. The apex always descends — never end at the highest point.
