FAILURE A — ALTERNATING BARS (destroys music completely)
  There is NO such thing as a "left hand bar" or a "right hand bar".
  Both hands play SIMULTANEOUSLY. Their notes go in the SAME BAR's note list.
  One bar = one moment in time = ALL voices together.

  FORBIDDEN:
    B1: 15 active pattern notes
    B2: D2,0,16              ← NOT "the left hand for bar 1"
    B3: melody notes
    B4: E2,0,16              ← NOT "the left hand for bar 3"
  Result: chunk → dead stop → chunk → dead stop. NOT music.

  CORRECT — every bar number appears EXACTLY ONCE, containing all voices:
    B1: A2,0,2; A3,2,2; E4,4,2; C4,6,2; A4,8,2; C4,10,2; E4,12,2; C4,14,2; E5,0,4; G5,4,2; F5,6,6; E5,12,4
    B2: D2,0,16; A3,2,2; F3,4,2; A3,6,2; D4,8,2; F3,10,2; A3,12,2; F3,14,2; F5,0,8; A5,8,4; G5,12,4
  Every bar number used exactly once. Left and right hand coexist in the same note list.

FAILURE B — SCALE RUNS (destroys melody)
  FORBIDDEN: C4,D4,E4,F4,G4,F4,E4,D4 repeated every phrase.
  If melody moves stepwise in ONE direction for more than 3 notes → STOP.
  Break it: add a leap, a direction change, a hold, or a rest.

FAILURE C — BOTH HANDS STOPPING TOGETHER
  NEVER write a bar where all voices go completely silent simultaneously (except the very final bar).
  That is a dead crash in the audio.
  For FAMILY 2 (split voice): the left hand pattern NEVER stops. If RH breathes, LH continues full pattern.
  For FAMILY 1 (unified sweep): the sweep never stops — it may thin out but never fully cuts.
  For FAMILY 3 (sparse lyrical): near-silence is allowed but always at least one voice sustains.

━━ STEP 0 — IDENTIFY TEXTURE FAMILY (do this first, everything else depends on it)

Look at the reference bars and count notes per bar, their pitch spread, and their d values.

FAMILY 1 — UNIFIED SWEEP ARPEGGIO (e.g. Evergreen / Two Steps From Hell style)
  Signs: 4-8 notes per bar, ALL same duration (all d=2 or all d=4), pitches span from low to very high,
  no clear register boundary between "left" and "right" hand — it's ONE flowing voice through all registers.
  Bass note at s=0 changes each bar following chord progression.
  Upper notes oscillate between a pair of pitches (e.g. E5↔G5, F5↔A5).
  → In this family: do NOT add a separate left hand pattern. The entire bar IS the texture.
    Replicate the oscillating sweep exactly. Bass note on s=0. Oscillating pair fills the rest.
    The "melody" is implied by which pair oscillates at the top.
    Energy arc = register climb (staircase) — same pattern, higher octave each section.

FAMILY 2 — SPLIT VOICE (LH engine + RH melody, e.g. Passacaglia / Einaudi)
  Signs: clear pitch register gap — low notes (oct 1-3) are dense/rhythmic, high notes (oct 4-6) are melodic/varied.
  LH has a repeating rhythmic cell (8 notes d=2, or shimmer d=1). RH has a singable phrase.
  → In this family: write LH rolling pattern continuously + RH melody floating above.
    See Priority 3 and 4 for full instructions.

FAMILY 3 — SPARSE LYRICAL (e.g. Gibran Alcocer / slow cinematic)
  Signs: very few notes per bar (2-5), long durations (d=8, d=12, d=16), wide empty space.
  Notes are placed with intention — each one carries emotional weight.
  → In this family: prioritise space and silence. Long held notes. Few but meaningful gestures.
    LH may be 2-3 long bass notes per bar. RH 1-3 notes, widely spaced.

FAMILY 4 — OSTINATO PULSE (e.g. fast rhythmic, driving)
  Signs: same pitch pattern repeating every 2-4 subdivisions, dense and mechanical.
  Pitches change but rhythm is locked. Feels hypnotic.
  → In this family: lock the rhythm absolutely. Vary only pitches. Never break the pulse.

IDENTIFY your reference as Family 1, 2, 3, or 4 before proceeding.

PRIORITY 2 — THE 12 LAWS OF GOOD MUSIC
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Good music = CONTROLLED INFORMATION FLOW through time.
NOT many notes. NOT complex theory. CONTROLLED FLOW.

GOOD MUSIC = MEMORY + EXPECTATION + VARIATION + CONTINUITY + ENERGY FLOW + EMOTIONAL TIMING

LAW 1 — IDENTITY
  Every piece needs recognisable DNA. Identity can be:
  a rhythm, a contour, a register behavior, a subdivision pattern, a harmonic movement — NOT only melody.
  Establish identity in bar 1. The listener must be able to hum or feel it by bar 2.

LAW 2 — REPETITION
  The brain LOVES prediction. Repeat your motif every 2-4 bars. Minimum 2 exact repetitions before first variation.
  Repetition creates: familiarity, safety, memory, recognition.

LAW 3 — VARIATION (one parameter at a time)
  Vary ONE parameter per cycle. Never all at once.
  Pitch / Rhythm / Register / Direction / Density / Ornament variation — ONE change per phrase cycle.

LAW 4 — CONTINUITY (music must feel inevitable)
  Every bar must feel like it HAD to follow from the previous bar.

LAW 5 — TENSION / RELEASE
  ONE chromatic note at the cadence point per 8-bar section = more emotion than 5 random chromatics.

LAW 6 — ENERGY FLOW
  Energy shapes: WAVE / PULSE / STAIRCASE / BREATHING / SPIRAL / COLLAPSE
  Plan this shape BEFORE writing any bars.

LAW 7 — MEMORY RECALL
  Return to the opening motif near the end. Recognition = closure.

LAW 8 — HIERARCHY (three levels simultaneously)
  MICRO: individual note choices. MESO: 2-4 bar phrases. MACRO: full piece arc.

LAW 9 — CONTRAST
  At least ONE strong contrast moment per piece.

LAW 10 — EMOTIONAL TIMING
  WHEN matters more than WHAT. A note after a rest hits harder than the same note without rest.

LAW 11 — FRACTAL (same geometry at different scales)
  Good music repeats its shape at micro, meso, and macro levels.

LAW 12 — INCOMPLETION (keep the listener leaning forward)
  Full resolution = final bar only.
