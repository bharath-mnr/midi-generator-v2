PRIORITY 0 — THREE HARD FORMAT FAILURES (FIX THESE FIRST)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

FAILURE A — ALTERNATING BARS (destroys music completely)
  There is NO such thing as a "left hand bar" or a "right hand bar".
  Both hands play SIMULTANEOUSLY. Their notes go in the SAME BAR's notes array.
  One bar = one moment in time = ALL voices together.

  FORBIDDEN:
    Bar 1: { 15 active pattern notes }
    Bar 2: { A2 d=16 }              ← NOT "the left hand for bar 1"
    Bar 3: { melody notes }
    Bar 4: { E2 d=16 }              ← NOT "the left hand for bar 3"
  Result: chunk → dead stop → chunk → dead stop. NOT music.

  CORRECT — every bar number appears EXACTLY ONCE, containing all voices:
    Bar 1 notes array: LH arpeggio notes (A2 A3 E4 C4...) + RH melody notes (E5 G5 F5...) — all together.
    Bar 2 notes array: LH arpeggio notes (D2 D3 A3 F3...) + RH breath hold (E5 d=15) — all together.
  Every bn used exactly once. Left and right hand coexist in the same notes list.

FAILURE B — SCALE RUNS (destroys melody)
  FORBIDDEN: C4→D4→E4→F4→G4→F4→E4→D4 repeated every phrase.
  If melody moves stepwise in ONE direction for more than 3 notes → STOP.
  Break it: add a leap, a direction change, a hold, or a rest.

FAILURE C — BOTH HANDS STOPPING TOGETHER
  NEVER write a bar where all voices go completely silent simultaneously (except the very final bar).
  That is a dead crash in the audio.
  For FAMILY 2 (split voice): the left hand pattern NEVER stops. If RH breathes, LH continues full pattern.
  For FAMILY 1 (unified sweep): the sweep never stops — it may thin out (fewer notes, longer d) but never fully cuts.
  For FAMILY 3 (sparse lyrical): near-silence is allowed but always at least one voice sustains.

━━ STEP 0 — IDENTIFY TEXTURE FAMILY (do this first, everything else depends on it)

Look at the reference bars and count notes per bar, their pitch spread, and their d values.
Then identify which texture family it belongs to:

FAMILY 1 — UNIFIED SWEEP ARPEGGIO (e.g. Evergreen / Two Steps From Hell style)
  Signs: 4-8 notes per bar, ALL same duration (all d=2 or all d=4), pitches span from low to very high,
  no clear register boundary between "left" and "right" hand — it's ONE flowing voice through all registers.
  Bass note at s=0 changes each bar following chord progression.
  Upper notes oscillate between a pair of pitches (e.g. E5↔G5, F5↔A5).
  → In this family: do NOT add a separate left hand pattern. The entire bar IS the texture.
    Replicate the oscillating sweep exactly. Bass note on s=0. Oscillating pair fills the rest.
    The "melody" is implied by which pair oscillates at the top.
    Energy arc = register climb (staircase) — same pattern, higher octave each section.

FAMILY 2 — SPLIT VOICE (LH engine + RH melody, e.g. Passacaglia / Einaudi)
  Signs: clear pitch register gap — low notes (oct 1-3) are dense/rhythmic, high notes (oct 4-6) are melodic/varied.
  LH has a repeating rhythmic cell (8 notes d=2, or shimmer d=1). RH has a singable phrase.
  → In this family: write LH rolling pattern continuously + RH melody floating above.
    See Priority 3 and 4 for full instructions.

FAMILY 3 — SPARSE LYRICAL (e.g. Gibran Alcocer / slow cinematic)
  Signs: very few notes per bar (2-5), long durations (d=8, d=12, d=16), wide empty space.
  Notes are placed with intention — each one carries emotional weight.
  → In this family: prioritise space and silence. Long held notes. Few but meaningful gestures.
    LH may be 2-3 long bass notes per bar. RH 1-3 notes, widely spaced.

FAMILY 4 — OSTINATO PULSE (e.g. fast rhythmic, driving)
  Signs: same pitch pattern repeating every 2-4 subdivisions, dense and mechanical.
  Pitches change but rhythm is locked. Feels hypnotic.
  → In this family: lock the rhythm absolutely. Vary only pitches. Never break the pulse.

IDENTIFY your reference as Family 1, 2, 3, or 4 before proceeding.
Then follow the instructions for that family throughout composition.

PRIORITY 2 — THE 12 LAWS OF GOOD MUSIC
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

Good music = CONTROLLED INFORMATION FLOW through time.
NOT many notes. NOT complex theory. CONTROLLED FLOW.

GOOD MUSIC = MEMORY + EXPECTATION + VARIATION + CONTINUITY + ENERGY FLOW + EMOTIONAL TIMING

LAW 1 — IDENTITY
  Every piece needs recognisable DNA. Identity can be:
  a rhythm, a contour, a register behavior, a subdivision pattern, a harmonic movement — NOT only melody.
  Passacaglia identity = melody note + pedal note alternation. That cell IS the identity.
  Establish identity in bar 1. The listener must be able to hum or feel it by bar 2.

LAW 2 — REPETITION
  The brain LOVES prediction. Repeat your motif every 2-4 bars. Minimum 2 exact repetitions before first variation.
  Repetition creates: familiarity, safety, memory, recognition.
  Pure repetition becomes dead — so use REPETITION + VARIATION, not one without the other.

LAW 3 — VARIATION (one parameter at a time)
  Vary ONE parameter per cycle. Never all at once.
  Pitch variation: same rhythm, different starting note (sequence / transposition)
  Rhythm variation: same contour, notes held longer or shorter
  Register variation: same motif one octave higher or lower
  Direction variation: ascending becomes descending (inversion)
  Density variation: 8 notes → 4 notes (same harmony, less motion)
  Ornament variation: add a grace note or trill to a familiar motif
  ONE change per phrase cycle. The listener recognises the old and feels the new.

LAW 4 — CONTINUITY (music must feel inevitable)
  Every bar must feel like it HAD to follow from the previous bar.
  Continuity methods:
    Overlap resolution: end of one phrase becomes start of next
    Voice leading: inner voices move by step or hold — never jump randomly
    Subdivision consistency: if LH uses d=2, keep d=2 through the section
    Register momentum: if pitch is rising over 4 bars, continue the arc
    Energy inertia: density builds gradually, not in sudden jumps
    Motif inheritance: each phrase borrows something from the last

LAW 5 — TENSION / RELEASE
  Music is emotional gravity. Without tension: boring. Without release: exhausting.
  ONE chromatic note at the cadence point per 8-bar section = more emotion than 5 random chromatics.
  Tension types: harmonic, rhythmic, density, register, silence, expectation, delay, chromatic.
  Passacaglia: G# and F# appear ONLY at cadence zones. That rarity makes them devastating.

LAW 6 — ENERGY FLOW
  Music is energy movement through time, not notes.
  The listener tracks: density, speed, motion, weight, register, brightness — as ENERGY.
  Energy shapes (choose one, commit to it):
    WAVE:      low → build → peak → fall → low
    PULSE:     stable pattern with density spikes at phrase boundaries
    STAIRCASE: motif migrates upward in register bar by bar across the piece
    BREATHING: motion → stillness → motion → stillness (Einaudi style)
    SPIRAL:    each cycle slightly more intense than the last
    COLLAPSE:  high energy → sudden sparse → rebuilds (dramatic reset)
  Plan this shape BEFORE writing any bars.

LAW 7 — MEMORY RECALL
  Good music recalls earlier material. This creates emotional nostalgia inside one track.
  Recall types: exact, mutated, rhythmic only, harmonic only, contour only, texture only.
  Return to the opening motif near the end. Even simplified or transformed — recognition = closure.

LAW 8 — HIERARCHY (three levels simultaneously)
  MICRO:  individual note choices and subdivision placements
  MESO:   2-4 bar phrases, each with a goal note they move toward
  MACRO:  full piece arc — one emotional story with a beginning, peak, and end
  All three must be coherent. Bad music only has micro ideas.

LAW 9 — CONTRAST
  Without contrast, the brain normalises everything. Contrast resets attention.
  At least ONE strong contrast moment per piece:
    high register → suddenly low / dense → suddenly sparse / fast inner motion → held chords
    Passacaglia: LH changes from 8 notes/bar to 2 sustained notes/bar — instant emotional shift.
  Through contrast, the left hand foundation STILL flows (unless this IS the LH contrast moment).

LAW 10 — EMOTIONAL TIMING
  WHEN matters more than WHAT. A note after a rest hits harder than the same note without rest.
  Timing tools: delayed resolution, lingering sustain, sudden interruption, micro pause,
  rhythmic suspension, breath timing, cadence spacing.
  Silence in the right hand is music architecture — not emptiness.

LAW 11 — FRACTAL (same geometry at different scales)
  Good music repeats its shape at multiple levels:
    small rise/fall → inside a larger rise/fall → inside the whole-track rise/fall.
  This creates cohesion without repetitiveness.
  Passacaglia: motif cycles + section cycles + harmony cycles + cadence cycles — all recursively aligned.

LAW 12 — INCOMPLETION (keep the listener leaning forward)
  Good music avoids full closure until very late.
  Methods: overlap continuation, unresolved cadence, delayed tonic, loop implication, forward motion.
  Every phrase ending should contain a thread that pulls into the next phrase.
  Full resolution = final bar only.

PRIORITY 3 — LEFT HAND ARCHITECTURE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

The left hand is the EARTH. Ground, gravity, pulse, harmonic stability, energy foundation.
Bad left hand destroys everything. Once it establishes a pattern, it maintains it.

LEFT HAND TYPES — choose based on style and reference DNA:

TYPE A — ROLLING BROKEN CHORD OSTINATO (most common, modern emotional piano)
  Pattern per bar (all d=2, 8 notes):
    s0=Root(oct2)  s2=Root(oct3)  s4=Fifth(oct3-4)  s6=Third(oct3)
    s8=Root(oct4)  s10=Third(oct3)  s12=Fifth(oct3-4)  s14=Third(oct3)
  Shape: ROOT ROOT FIFTH THIRD ROOT THIRD FIFTH THIRD
  Creates: constant emotional motion, listener feels it as one steady pulse.
  Example for Am: A2 A3 E4 C4 A4 C4 E4 C4 — all s values, all d=2.

TYPE B — LONG SUSTAIN BASS (cinematic, spacious)
  One bass note held, d=8 or d=16. Root on s=0. Right hand moves freely above.
  Creates: space, weight, calm. Used when RH is very active.
  Can also be an octave pair: root(oct1) + root(oct2) both held d=12.

TYPE C — PULSE OCTAVES (driving, momentum)
  Low octave hits repeated rhythmically. Root+octave, d=2 or d=4, every beat.
  s=0 and s=8 = half-time feel. s=0,4,8,12 = quarter-note drive.
  Creates: forward momentum, urgency.

TYPE D — PASSACAGLIA GROUND (fixed repeating harmonic cycle)
  Exact same harmonic loop every N bars. Right hand evolves above it.
  Listener always knows "home" — the ground never surprises.
  Very powerful psychologically. Use when the piece needs maximum anchor.

TYPE E — SHIMMER (Einaudi/Primavera style)
  All d=1, 8 sixteenth notes per bar, c=88 on each (detached).
  Pattern: root4 fifth4 root5 fifth4 / root4 fifth4 root5 fifth4
  Creates: floating shimmer, light and airy texture.
  Root gets c=96 for slight emphasis.

TYPE F — RHYTHMIC COUNTERFLOW
  LH rhythm intentionally different from RH.
  If RH is on beats → LH is on offbeats. Creates temporal depth.

COMMON BROKEN CHORD SHAPES (for any LH type using arpeggiation):
  1-5-8-5   (most common, balanced)
  1-3-5-8   (warmer, fuller)
  1-5-10-5  (very cinematic, wide)
  1-5-8-10  (uplifting, expansive)

ONE VARIABLE RULE:
  When LH changes texture → RH stays simple.
  When RH is active → LH keeps exact pattern.
  Never change both simultaneously.

PRIORITY 4 — RIGHT HAND ARCHITECTURE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

The right hand controls: emotion, motion, identity, attention.
Its job is to carry the motif and transform it over time.

RIGHT HAND TYPES:

TYPE A — MELODY + PEDAL (Passacaglia style)
  One voice traces a melody, another voice holds a fixed pedal note.
  Alternation pattern: moving note at s=0, anchor at s=2, moving at s=4, anchor at s=6...
  Creates: hypnotic weave, one voice "orbiting" the other.

TYPE B — BROKEN CHORD FLOW (Gibran Alcocer, Einaudi)
  Pattern: low → mid → high → mid, repeated.
  Pitch changes with harmony. Rhythm stays constant.
  Creates: water flow motion, continuous forward feeling.

TYPE C — OSTINATO RHYTHM (listener memory lock)
  Fixed rhythm repeats. Only pitches change.
  Very effective: brain locks onto the rhythm and tracks pitch changes emotionally.

TYPE D — SUSTAIN + MOTION HYBRID (cinematic)
  One long emotional note held. Small motion underneath or alongside.
  Creates: held breath feeling. Very emotional.

TYPE E — OCTAVE PARALLEL MOTION (climax)
  Same melody note played in two octaves simultaneously.
  Creates: weight, power, cinematic gravity.
  Save for climax or emotional peak.

MELODY QUALITY RULES:
  Simple contour. Strong identity. Controlled repetition. Memorable rhythm. Small mutations.
  Stepwise motion (2nds and 3rds) more than leaps — feels vocal, human, emotional.
  After 3 stepwise notes in one direction → change direction, leap, hold, or rest.
  Goal notes: each phrase moves TOWARD a specific pitch and arrives there.
  Question-answer pairs: phrase 1 ends on unstable degree (2,4,5,7), phrase 2 resolves (1,3,5).

MOTIF FORMULA: IDENTITY + SPACE + REPETITION + SMALL SURPRISE
BAD MELODY: too random, too many jumps, too dense, no repetition, no contour identity.

PRIORITY 5 — BREATH, REST, AND SILENCE (STYLE-DEPENDENT)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

NOT every track needs explicit breath moments. This depends on the style and your reference DNA.

Continuous flow tracks (Passacaglia Pattern A, ostinato textures, pulse-driven pieces):
  The right hand moves continuously for many bars. No explicit breath moments.
  Energy and phrase identity come from pitch variation and register shifts — not from rests.

Lyrical/sparse tracks (Primavera-style, Gibran Alcocer, slow cinematic):
  The right hand does breathe — long holds, silence moments, sparse single notes.
  These arrive at predictable phrase boundaries (every 4, 6, or 8 bars) — not randomly.

IF your style calls for breath, use one of these types (all with LH continuing underneath):
  LONG HOLD:      RH holds one note d=8 to d=15 over a full bar
  CHROMATIC SIGH: RH plays 3-5 slow chromatic notes ending on a hold (Passacaglia B-pattern)
  SPARSE TOUCH:   RH plays 1-2 notes only in a bar (single word before silence)
  RH SILENT:      RH notes=[] for 1 bar — LH continues FULL pattern with all notes

THE ONE ABSOLUTE RULE (applies to ALL styles):
  If the right hand breathes or stops → the left hand NEVER acknowledges it.
  The left hand keeps its full texture pattern through every breath bar.
  Dead silence in both hands simultaneously = audio crash. Never do this.

PRIORITY 6 — SECTION ARCHITECTURE AND TRANSITIONS
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

SECTION FLOW (most common modern piano structure):
  INTRO       → establish the ground (LH only, or very sparse RH)
  ESTABLISH   → motif stated clearly, texture set
  DEVELOP     → motif varied (pitch/direction/register), energy builds
  EXPAND      → new counter-melody OR register push OR density increase
  CONTRAST    → strong texture change (one variable only)
  RELEASE     → sparse, breathing, emotional exhale
  RECALL      → return to opening material (transformed or simplified)
  RESOLVE     → final arrival, tonic, stillness

Bad arrangement: same energy forever. Brain adapts → boredom.
Good arrangement: energy evolves continuously, but always feels inevitable.

SECTION EVOLUTION METHODS (use one per section change):
  register rise, density increase, new counter melody, octave doubling,
  rhythmic mutation, texture widening, harmonic brightening, LH texture change

SMOOTH TRANSITION METHODS (transitions separate amateurs from masters):
  OVERLAP:             old section continues slightly into new section
  SHARED NOTE:         one note belongs to both sections — psychological bridge
  REGISTER BRIDGE:     new section begins near old section's register
  RHYTHMIC INHERITANCE: rhythm stays the same, harmony changes
  ENERGY PREPARATION:  new section hinted 1-2 bars before it arrives

PRIORITY 7 — DENSITY, REGISTER, SYNCOPATION
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

DENSITY THEORY:
  Density = notes per unit time.
  MORE NOTES ≠ MORE EMOTION. Sometimes LESS density creates MORE emotion.
  Density shapes: stable / expanding / contracting / exploding / breathing
  Plan your density arc. Rarely keep density flat the entire piece.

REGISTER THEORY (emotional geography):
  LOW (C1–B2):   heavy, dark, dangerous, grounded
  MID-LOW (C3–B3): warm, stable, foundational
  MID (C4–B4):   human, intimate, natural
  HIGH (C5–B5):  memory, dream, fragility, distance
  VERY HIGH (C6+): ethereal, tension, ecstatic
  Great music moves between register zones gradually. Register storytelling.
  Register arc: where does the melody START, PEAK, and END?

SYNCOPATION THEORY (modern emotional piano uses SOFT syncopation):
  Expected: beat 1 2 3 4 (s=0,4,8,12)
  Soft syncopation: delayed hit, early hit, missing beat, off-grid emphasis
  Example: melody enters at s=2 instead of s=0 → emotional hesitation
  Example: melody enters at s=14 (last 16th) → sweeps into next bar as pickup
  Use sparingly — one syncopated entry per phrase feels expressive, many feels chaotic.

MATHEMATICAL BALANCE (hidden structure the brain feels):
  2-bar loops, 4-bar loops, 6-bar loops, 8-bar loops — brain compresses them easily.
  Phrase symmetry, section recursion, repetition intervals, cadence timing.
  The "grid" should be invisible but felt — like a heartbeat, not a metronome announcement.

ARTICULATION GUIDE:
  c = end_cutoff_percent:
    Omit c   → legato / full sustain (melody long tones, bass pedals)
    c:88     → shimmer (Einaudi LH sixteenth style)
    c:70     → slightly detached (inner chord notes)
    c:50     → portato (accented melody notes)
    c:38     → warm detached (Primavera melody style)
    c:30     → staccato (light bouncy notes)

  o = offset_percent (micro-timing, human feel):
    Omit o   → straight grid
    o:15–20  → subtle human feel on melody notes
    o:30–40  → swing feel on eighth notes
